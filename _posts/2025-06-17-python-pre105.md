## **OOP** 
**Object-Oriented Programming (OOP)** "bring" the real world into digital world, abstracting real-life concepts and problems into a digital representation that a computer can process. 

OOP has four *characteristics* with the first two introduced later. 
- **Encapsulation** 
- **Inheritance** 
- **Polymorphism**
- **Abstraction**.

Before OOP, we had **procedural programming** utilizing functions and basic data structures discussed previously. Functions must be defined in the order that they are used. This leads to its drawback which is the ability to manage a large interconnecting system like social media platforms with so many users and relationships among them. 

OOP addresses this issue by grouping **objects** (subjects) with similar **attributes** (properties) and **methods** (behaviors). We name such a group a **class**. We'll discuss these concepts in detail in the following sections. 

Take library management as an example. *Classes* can be books, readers, and librarians; each book, reader, and librarian is an *object* of its class. Each reader should have *attributes* like name, ID, and contact method. In addition, we need a process of borrowing and returning books. This process is associated with *methods* of a class. A librarian can lend books and take returns from readers.

    ...
