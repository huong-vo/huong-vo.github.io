**Why not $n$?** <br>
The sample variance calculated by dividing by $n$ is often referred to as the **naive sample variance**. The problem with it is that we would *consistently underestimate* the variance around the population mean. This happens because, *on average* across all possible samples drawn from the population, the naive sample variance is *smaller* than the population variance. *Mathematically*, we have 

$$\begin{align}
\label{naive-inequality-problem}
E \left( \frac{1}{n} \sum_{k=1}^{n} (x_k - \bar{x})^2 \right) < \frac{1}{N} \sum_{k=1}^{N} (x_k - \mu)^2.
\end{align}$$

1. Let's first understand what each side of this equality means.
    - The *right-hand side* is simply the population variance. 

    $$\frac{1}{N} \sum (x_k - \mu)^2 = \sigma^2$$

    - The *left-hand side* is the expected value of all possible naive sample variances, particularly the mean in this case. Here, we are *not* just comparing a single sample's naive variance. *Instead*, we want to analyze the relation between the naive sample variance and the entire population variance overall, that is, from a global point of view. 

    We have the left-hand side can be rewritten as

    $$E \left( \frac{1}{n} \sum_{k=1}^{n} (x_k - \bar{x})^2 \right) = E(s^2).$$

2. Before working out the detail, let't see how this idea might work in the case of a single sample's naive variance and why we need the average. 
    - *Claim:* $$\sum_{i=1}^{n} (x_k - \bar{x})^2 < \sum_{i=1}^{N} (x_k - \mu)^2 \text{ for any } \bar{x}$$.

    - *Proof*: According the claim, we want to minimize the sum of squared deviations. The common method is to figure out the absolute minimum, in particular local minimum in this case. 
    
        Consider $f(a) = \sum_{k=1}^{n} (x_k - a)^2$. Differentiating the function, we have

        $$\begin{align*}
        \frac{df}{da} = \frac{d}{da} \sum_{k=1}^{n} (x_k - a)^2 = -2 \sum_{k=1}^{n} (x_k - a).
        \end{align*}$$

        The local minimum of the function is at 

        $$\begin{align}
        &-2 \sum_{k=1}^{n} (x_k - a) = 0 \Rightarrow \sum_{k=1}^{n} (x_k - a) = 0 \\
        &\Rightarrow \sum_{k=1}^{n} x_k = \sum_{k=1}^{n} a = na \Rightarrow a = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n} x_k}{n} = \bar{x} \\
        \end{align}$$




Let's go inside out. Since we want to compare with the population variance, we want to involve $\mu$ in the left side of \eqref{naive-inequality-problem}. 

$$\begin{align}
(x_k - \bar{x})^2 &= ((x_k - \mu) - (\bar{x} - \mu))^2 \nonumber \\
                  &= (x_k - \mu)^2 - 2(x_k - \mu)(\bar{x} - \mu) + (\bar{x} - \mu)^2. \label{bar_x-to-mu}
\end{align}$$

Using \eqref{bar_x-to-mu}, we have

$$\begin{align}
\sum (x_k - \bar{x})^2 &= \sum (x_k - \mu)^2 - \sum 2(x_k - \mu)(\bar{x} - \mu) + \sum (\bar{x} - \mu)^2 \nonumber \\
                       &= \sum (x_k - \mu)^2 - 2(\bar{x} - \mu) \sum (x_k - \mu) + n (\bar{x} - \mu)^2. \label{sum-bar_x-to-mu}
\end{align}$$

We know that 

$$\begin{align}
\label{x_k-mu}
\sum (x_k - \mu) = \sum x_k - \sum \mu = n \bar{x} - n \mu = n (\bar{x} - \mu)
\end{align}$$

Substitute \eqref{x_k-mu} into \eqref{sum-bar_x-to-mu}. We have

$$\begin{align}
\sum (x_k - \bar{x})^2 &= \sum (x_k - \mu)^2 - 2(\bar{x} - \mu) n (\bar{x} - \mu) + n (\bar{x} - \mu)^2 \nonumber \\
                       &= \sum (x_k - \mu)^2 - 2n (\bar{x} - \mu)^2 + n (\bar{x} - \mu)^2 \nonumber \\
                       &= \sum (x_k - \mu)^2 - n (\bar{x} - \mu)^2. \label{result-bar_x-to-mu}
\end{align}$$

Substituting \eqref{result-bar_x-to-mu} into the left side of \eqref{naive-inequality-problem}, we have

$$\begin{align}
E \left( \frac{1}{n} \sum (x_k - \bar{x})^2 \right) &= E \left(\frac{1}{n} \left[\sum (x_k - \mu)^2 - n (\bar{x} - \mu)^2 \right] \right) \nonumber \\
                                                    &= E \left(\frac{1}{n} \left[\sum (x_k - \mu)^2 \right] \right) - E \left(\frac{1}{n} n (\bar{x} - \mu)^2 \right) \nonumber \\
                                                    &= \frac{1}{n} \sum [E ((x_k - \mu)^2)] - E ((\bar{x} - \mu)^2) \label{result-expectation-naive-sample-variance}
\end{align}$$

By definition of the general variance, we have

$$
\begin{align}
&E ((\bar{x} - \mu)^2) = var(\bar{X}) = var \left(\frac{1}{n} \sum x_k \right) \\
&E ((\bar{x})^2) - \mu^2 
\end{align}$$


$$\frac{\sum (x - \bar{x})^2}{n} < \frac{\sum (x - \mu)^2}{N}.$$
