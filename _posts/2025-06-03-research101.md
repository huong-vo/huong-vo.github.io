## How to Find Scientific Documents/Papers
Having the right materials to read up besides textbooks or lecture notes is crucial while you study or research any subject. However, such materials do not just magically appear in front of us. You need to not only dig through the internet for them but make sure their origins are reliable as well. 

There are two primary types of resources that scholars use to suppport their projects including published literature and preprints. We will now talk about where to obtain them and what their differences are in terms of reliability.
    
### Published Literature
Published literature refers to peer-reviewed sources which are formally released by recognized publishing companies and made available to the public. Here are several places where such documents/papers in the field of AI can be found using keywords.

- **[Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/)** is the first go-to webiste for researchers of *all* areas. This is a fast and straightforward way to learn about articles suitable for your research works. The website provides the sources of most (if not all) academic papers with different versions or publishers and related information. 
You can narrow down results by using year filters. Say, when you are looking for a literature review/survey since it's recommneded at papers printed within the last 3-5 years. 

- **[IEEE](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/Xplore/home.jsp)** contains scientific and technical documents issued by itself and its partners. Using this website, you can look for related publications based on its content type such as conferences and journals, its affiliation,and many more. 

- **[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/)** is a free database for those interested in using AI in biomedical and life sciences. Readers can While full-text archives by PubMed Central (PMC) are free, access to other publishers' articles may require a fee.

- **Other Resources:** Trusted publishers such as [Springer](https://link.springer.com/), [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/), and [Elsevier](https://www.elsevier.com/) provide access to scientific papers across various field. 


### **Preprints**
Even though published literature are validated resources, the peer review and editting process can take about 3-6 months or even a year or more. It means researchers do not have full accesss to up-to-date techniques, which delays the whole research community's progress. With that in mind, scholars created sites allowing people to post their research works before or while they are submitted to a journal or conference. 

Keep in mind that these papers may introduce the lastest methods, but they might not have been evaluated by experts yet. Here are a few popular places where preprints can be checked out.

- **[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/)** offers more than 2 million free scholarly articles in the field of physics, mathematics, computer science, quantitative biology, quantitative finance, statistics, electrical engineering and systems science, and economics.  

- **[bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/)** specializes in biology and life sciences. Formal version of many articles are updated weeks after their published date.

- **[Papers with Code](https://paperswithcode.com/)** is a free and open resource specifically for Machine Learning papers. Even though the site does not require the corresponding code implementations upon submission, source code and datasets are suggested being included. This is a great way for readers to understand, reproduce, and improve ideas and experiments presented in these research articles.

## **How to Read Scientific Documents/Papers**
While both scientific papers and essays heavily involve writing, it's worth noting that they are quite disparate. In few words, scientific papers are factual whereas essays' tone is biased. The table below comprises key differences between the two. 

|    | Scientific Papers | Essays |
|----|-------------------|--------|
| **Purpose** | contribute new knowledge to a specific field | argue a point, explore a topic, or reflect on an issue |
| **Target audience** | scholars, researchers, scientists, and professionals | general audience or special readers |
| **Focus** | empirical evidence, methodologies, and data analysis | critical thinking, analysis, and personal perspectives |
| **Structure** | introduction, literature review, methodology, findings, and conclusion | introduction, body, and conclusion |

<br>

:pushpin: Reading a research paper can be challenging, especially for first-timers; it can even take them a month to understand one. It is common to struggle with *new* terms and algorithms since an absolute beginner needs to learn from references. **Don't be discouraged**; it gets easier over time. Your reading pace and comprehension will improve if you're consistent. 

If you are totally new to research (like me), you may wonder: Should I read the whole paper? If not, which parts should I skip? Should I try to understand every single detail or aim to get the big picture? 

:question: All these boil down to one concern: **How do I read a research paper efficiently?** We'll now discuss Dr. Vinh Dinh's reading technique based on his first-hand experience step by step. 

1. **Title:** The *very first* thing you should do when picking up a paper is to read its title. The title consists of keywords that helps determine whether a paper matches your research topic or not. If the title has or is related to the name of ideas or techniques you are interested in, you can move to the next step. Otherwise, you can pass. 

2. **Abstract:** Once you're good to move forward, read its abstract. The abstract is considered the essense of an article, summarizing every remaining section within 250-300 words. It enables you quickly assess its relevance and value to your work so you can decide if the paper is worth your effort. 

3. **Introduction:** If the abstract is intriguing, start reading the introduction section. The introduction shows you the big piture of what this research is about. Here you'll learn about the research context and questions which all help layout the paper's structure and main arguments. Basically, this is where scholars sell their papers.

4. **Experimental Result:** It is *imperative* that you read this section right after the introduction because how they advertise their "product" and how good their ideas are may not be what you are looking for. You'd like to know if what the authors propose is actually practicable in your case and provides the result meets your expectations. This section may contain data visualizations and statistics helping you make a decision. The idea is for you to skip some long parts of the paper that may be unnecessary to you. 

5. **Proposed Method:** If you're satisfied with the result, you can go back to the proposed method to learn how they produce, brainstorm, and implement their innovative ideas to resolve their research problem. Note that it may take up to a couple of weeks to grasp the picture. Also, you won't, most of the times, be able to comprehend their method immediately. That's when you read the related works or look up references. 

6. **Related Works:** This section is a survey of previously related works and their drawbacks. Readers can refer to this part and the *Reference* section to obtain relevant knowledge, techniques, and algorithms.

Similar to reading a scientific paper, you can use AI to learn AI when it comes to making a research plan. ## **Where to Implement Your Code**
You have ideas; you want to put it into action. Here are a couple of places where you can do so.

- **[Jupyter Notebook](https://jupyter.org/):** You can use it via either [Python Compiler](https://www.python.org/downloads/) or [Anaconda](https://www.anaconda.com/download). The latter one has various tools in addition to Jupyter Notebook and is easier to install. Since this is run locally and AI training requires GPUs with high VRAM, you computer's system resources might not be sufficient. Google Colab, which is mentioned below, can resolve this issue to a large extent.

- **[Google Colab](https://colab.google/):** Google Colab provides free limited (but enough to start with) online resources. You just need to connect with your gmail account and begin using it without any installation. This site allows you to mount your notebooks to Google Drive or Github and download/upload them to/from your computer and has built-in Gemini AI. In Colab, your code runs on CPU by default; however, you can change runtime type to GPU or TPU.

### **AI Coding Assistants**
In addition to [Google Gemini](https://gemini.google.com/app), consider using [ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com/) and [Microsoft Copilot](https://copilot.microsoft.com/chats/msspsHbRm7fEf3swc7ke9), and what they can do for you. Of course, this again requires your prompting skills. 

- **Generate a sample code:** "Generate a Python example that uses Matplotlib and plots a square function from -10 to 10."
- **Explain** an existing code *step by step*.
- **Add comments** to an existing code.
- **Debug** an existing code and **suggest a solution:** "This program has anunexpected result. Find it out."
- **Design a solution for a program** based on a prompt or paper: "I have an ID array of numbers. I want to get themaximum number. Design an algorithm and give apseudo-code using Pythonic style."
- **Explain theory/concepts:** "What is theLambda function in Python?"

I encourage all of us try prompts out with AI and see how it performs and how you can make it work better for us.  

:warning: While AI assistants can help you learn coding faster, *overreliance* on AI can *reduce* your critical thinking and programming skills if you first learn coding.

> You should *verify* anything AI assistants provide. They are *just* your assistants! They can be wrong. <br>
> -Dr. Vinh Dinh Nguyen

## **How to Document Your Results**
While you can use Microsoft Word to make a document, it's probably universal that scholars document their research works using **LaTeX**, which is more convenient for professionals to create scientific and technical content. 

- You can download softwares like [MikTex](https://miktex.org/) and [TeXworks](https://miktex.org/) (included in MikTex's package) to use and preview offline. 

- You can also write LaTex online using [Overleaf](https://cs.overleaf.com/). The perk of using this is that it allows multiple collaborators to edit on the same file simultaneously.
