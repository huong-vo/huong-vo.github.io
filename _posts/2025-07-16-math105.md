Descriptive Statistics refers to tools that summarize and describe features of a dataset, providing us with a clearer picture of the data. There are three main categories: central tendency, variability, and distribution.

## **Measure of Central Tendency**
### **Mean and Expected Value**
Expected value is not considered a measure of central tendency, but this concept and mean are closely related as they both represent some sort of average.

### **Median**
Given a dataset of N items $X = \\{X_1, \cdots, X_N\\}$, median is the value separating the sorted dataset in half. To obtain the median, we follow two steps. 
1. Sort $X$.
2. Calculate the median $m$:
    - If $N$ is odd, then $m = X_{\frac{N + 1}{2}}$.
    - If $N$ is even, then $m = \frac{X_{\frac{N}{2}} + X_{\frac{N + 1}{2}}}{2}$.

## **Measure of Variability**
### **Variance and Standard Deviation** 
<table class = "language-python">
    <tr>
        <th>Variance</th>
        <th>Standard Deviation</th>
    </tr>
    <tr>
        <td colspan = "2" class = "centered_td">
        measure <strong>how spread out</strong> data in a given set are from its mean
        </td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
        <td>$$\sigma^2 = var(X) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (X_i - \mu)^2$$</td>
        <td>$$\sigma = \sqrt{var(X)}$$</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
        <td>
            <ul>
                <li> the average of squared differences from the mean </li>
                <li> squared distance between data points and the mean </li>
                <li> measured in squared units </li>
            </ul>
        </td>
        <td>
            <ul>
                <li> the square root of the variance </li>
                <li> typical distance between data points and the mean </li>
                <li> measured in a unit similar to the mean's unit </li>
            </ul>
        </td>
    </tr>
</table>
